## Research Identity

I am an applied statistician developing statistical methodology for **complex systems**, where uncertainty, heterogeneity, and changing data-generating processes challenge conventional statistical assumptions.

My work spans quantitative finance, economics, scientific computing, biomedical imaging, and other data-intensive domains. I am less interested in any single application area than in the methodological problems that recur across them:

> **How can statistical inference remain reliable when the assumptions underlying our models no longer hold?**

I study this question by combining **statistical modelling, econometrics, machine learning, uncertainty quantification, and model validation**. My work focuses on identifying hidden structure, validating models under realistic conditions, and developing methods that remain robust when data are heterogeneous, nonstationary, partially observed, or affected by measurement uncertainty.

### Hidden Regimes of Financial Instability
#### *A Finite Mixture Modelling Approach to Systemic Banking Crises*

  <img src="https://img.shields.io/badge/Status-Under%20Review-475569?style=flat-square"/>

**Problem**

Can systemic banking crises be explained by a single global model, or do they emerge from distinct latent macro-financial regimes?

**Contribution**

Developed a finite-mixture framework that identifies latent crisis regimes before estimating regime-specific econometric models. The project examines whether pooled crisis equations obscure structurally different mechanisms of financial instability.

**Methods**

### The Illusion of Predictability
#### *Structural Heterogeneity and Informational Noise in Corporate Greenwashing*

<p>
  <img src="https://img.shields.io/badge/Status-Under%20Review-475569?style=flat-square"/>
  <img src="https://img.shields.io/badge/Domain-Corporate%20Prediction-0f172a?style=flat-square"/>

  <img src="https://img.shields.io/badge/R-0f172a?style=flat-square&logo=r&logoColor=white"/>
</p>

**Problem**

When predictive performance is weak, does it reflect an inadequate statistical model or an intrinsic limit of the observable information?

**Contribution**

Combined global regression, model-based recursive partitioning, and repeated out-of-sample validation to study parameter instability and weak generalization. The project treats low predictability not simply as model failure, but as potential evidence of fragmented or unstable information.

**Methods**

`Model-Based Recursive Partitioning` `Bootstrap Validation` `Out-of-Sample Testing` `Predictive Modelling` `Model Risk`

### [PanelScope](https://github.com/Ma-Mar-Itan/PanelScope)

A statistical software tool for constructing coverage-consistent panel datasets before econometric estimation.

PanelScope identifies the largest jointly observable set of variables satisfying a user-defined sample-retention threshold. It makes the trade-off between model complexity and data availability explicit, reproducible, and inspectable before modelling begins.

**Why it matters**

Empirical panel datasets often lose observations when variables are combined. PanelScope helps researchers document that loss, evaluate coverage by entity and year, and export a defensible analysis sample.

**Highlights**

- exact and heuristic optimization
- panel-data coverage diagnostics
- year and entity retention analysis
- Excel, CSV, Parquet, and JSON reporting
- automated testing and validation

**Stack:** Python · Streamlit · Optimization · Panel Data · Testing

</td>

<td width="50%" valign="top">

### [Wazir](https://github.com/Ma-Mar-Itan/Project_Wazir)

A modular personal AI assistant designed around contextual decision support rather than static task management.

The current Oracle module dynamically prioritizes tasks using long-term goals, current constraints, energy level, and recent decision history. It is built as a local-first AI system with Telegram as the interface and Google Sheets as the auditable persistence layer.

**Why it matters**

Most task systems store priorities as fixed labels. Wazir treats priority as a changing decision problem that should be recomputed from context.

**Highlights**

- adaptive task ranking
- contextual memory
- Telegram text and voice interface
- LLM-assisted reasoning
- modular assistant architecture

**Stack:** Python · Telegram API · OpenRouter · Google Sheets · LLM Systems

</td>

### [BibBrief](https://github.com/Ma-Mar-Itan/BibBrief)

A research utility that converts large BibTeX libraries into structured Microsoft Word documents for literature screening.

BibBrief is designed for systematic reviews, scoping reviews, thesis work, and research assistant workflows where raw bibliography files need to become readable documents.

**Why it matters**

Large literature searches often produce hundreds or thousands of references. BibBrief turns them into a document that can be reviewed, annotated, and distributed.

**Highlights**

- robust BibTeX parsing
- Word document generation
- duplicate detection
- scalable processing for large bibliographies
- no AI rewriting or paraphrasing

**Stack:** Python · Streamlit · python-docx · Research Automation

</td>

<td width="50%" valign="top">

### ET-Sim

A metadata-only discrete-event simulator for large-scale scientific workflows on heterogeneous computing resources.

ET-Sim represents petabyte-scale logical workloads through metadata rather than physical files, allowing scheduling policies to be tested on a laptop while preserving simulated runtime, queueing, I/O pressure, uncertainty, and deadline risk.

**Why it matters**

It provides a lightweight research platform for studying uncertainty-aware scheduling, admission control, and overload behaviour in scientific workflow systems.

**Highlights**

- discrete-event simulation
- CPU/GPU/I/O resource modelling
- uncertainty-aware scheduling
- admission control and backfilling
- reproducible synthetic workloads

**Stack:** Python · Simulation · Scheduling · Scientific Computing · Uncertainty

## Writing

### [Principia Statistica](https://github.com/Ma-Mar-Itan/principia-statistica-web)
#### *The Intuitive Foundations of Mathematical Evidence, Inference, and Risk*

*Principia Statistica* is a book in progress on the conceptual foundations of statistical reasoning.

Rather than teaching statistics as a sequence of formulas, the book develops the ideas behind statistical inference: uncertainty, evidence, variability, signal and noise, measurement, risk, prediction, and the distinction between data and understanding.

The accompanying repository hosts an interactive web edition built with **Next.js**, **MDX**, and reusable figure components.

**Themes**

`Statistical Reasoning` `Evidence` `Uncertainty` `Inference` `Risk` `Scientific Communication`

## Research Principles

- Build methods that remain useful when ideal assumptions fail.
- Treat validation as part of modelling, not an afterthought.
- Prefer robust inference over optimistic prediction.
- Use predictive failure as a source of scientific information.
- Distinguish signal from artifacts before interpreting structure.
- Make research software reproducible, inspectable, and useful beyond one dataset.

> *The aim of my research is to develop statistical methods that remain reliable when data-generating processes are heterogeneous, uncertain, or evolving.*
